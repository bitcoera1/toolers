What Is GST / VAT?

GST (Goods and Services Tax) and VAT (Value Added Tax) are forms of consumption tax applied to many goods and services. Depending on the country or jurisdiction, the tax may be added to a base price or already included in the displayed price.

For example, if an item costs $100 before tax and an 18% tax rate applies, the tax amount is $18 and the tax-inclusive price becomes $118.

Quick Example

Base Price = $100

Tax Rate = 18%

Tax Amount = $18

Final Price = $118

How to Add GST / VAT to a Price

When the entered amount is the price before tax, calculate the tax as a percentage of the base price and then add it to the original amount.

Tax Amount = Base Price × (Tax Rate ÷ 100)
Final Price = Base Price + Tax Amount

For example, with a base price of $1,000 and a tax rate of 18%, the tax amount is $180, producing a final price of $1,180.

If you need to calculate a percentage separately before applying tax, use the ToolXone Percentage Calculator to calculate percentages, percentage increases, and percentage decreases.

How to Remove GST / VAT From a Tax-Inclusive Price

If GST or VAT is already included in a price, finding the original price is different from simply subtracting the tax percentage from the total. The tax-inclusive amount must be divided by the tax multiplier.

Base Price = Tax-Inclusive Price ÷ (1 + Tax Rate ÷ 100)
Tax Amount = Tax-Inclusive Price − Base Price

For example, if $1,180 already includes 18% tax, the base price is $1,000 and the included tax amount is $180.

Tax and discounts affect prices differently. If you are working with a sale price or promotional reduction, use the ToolXone Discount Calculator to calculate the discount amount, savings, and final discounted price.

Important: Removing 18% tax from a tax-inclusive price is not the same as reducing that price by 18%. The tax was calculated from the original pre-tax amount.

Tax-Inclusive vs Tax-Exclusive Prices

A tax-exclusive price is the price before GST, VAT, or another percentage tax is added. A tax-inclusive price already contains the tax.

Knowing which type of amount you have is important because adding tax and extracting tax require different calculations.

GST vs VAT: What Is the Difference?

GST and VAT are both terms used for consumption-tax systems, but their names, rates, exemptions, reporting requirements, and collection rules can differ between countries and jurisdictions.
